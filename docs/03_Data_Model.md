\# StoryOS Data Model Specification



\## Introduction



The purpose of this document is to define the building blocks of every universe created inside StoryOS.



The previous document explained how StoryOS is organised as a collection of independent systems.



This document explains what those systems are responsible for storing.



Everything that exists inside StoryOS should belong to one clearly defined category.



Doing this keeps the software organised and prevents information from becoming duplicated or contradictory.



StoryOS should always know where information belongs.



If a piece of information exists, there should always be one authoritative place where it is stored.



This makes the universe easier to manage, easier to search, and easier for the AI to understand.



\---



\# 1. The Universe



The Universe is the highest level of every project.



Every story belongs to a universe.



A universe represents a completely independent reality.



Two universes should never accidentally share information.



A fantasy universe should remain separate from a science-fiction universe unless the user deliberately connects them.



A universe contains the foundations upon which every story is built.



This includes:



\* The world's history.

\* Its timeline.

\* Geography.

\* Technology level.

\* Magic system, if one exists.

\* Important historical events.

\* The countries and territories that exist.

\* The factions that operate within it.

\* Every character that belongs to that world.



A universe should feel like a living place rather than simply being a folder that stores chapters.



\---



\# 2. Countries and Territories



Countries and territories form the political, cultural and geographical framework of a universe.



Stories happen somewhere.



Characters grow up somewhere.



Wars happen somewhere.



Every location should therefore possess its own identity.



Each country or territory should be capable of storing information such as:



\* Government.

\* Culture.

\* Economy.

\* Geography.

\* Population.

\* Military strength.

\* History.

\* Current political situation.

\* Relationships with other countries.



Characters should be influenced by the places they live.



Someone raised in a peaceful republic should naturally think differently from someone raised inside an authoritarian dictatorship.



The world shapes the people living inside it.



\---



\# 3. Teams, Families and Factions



StoryOS treats organised groups as important entities in their own right.



A team is not merely a collection of characters.



It possesses its own history, traditions, culture and identity.



Examples include:



\* Hero organisations.

\* Biological families.

\* Royal houses.

\* Criminal syndicates.

\* Military units.

\* Political parties.

\* Secret societies.

\* Religious organisations.



Each faction should contain information such as:



\* Name.

\* Purpose.

\* History.

\* Beliefs.

\* Leadership.

\* Organisational structure.

\* Internal culture.

\* Public reputation.

\* Relationships with other factions.



Membership of a faction should influence how its members behave.



The same individual may act differently depending on whether they are speaking as a family member, a soldier, or a political representative.



\---



\# 4. Characters



Characters are the individuals who inhabit the universe.



Every character should feel like a unique person rather than a reusable AI personality.



StoryOS should store far more than a name and appearance.



A character should include:



Their identity.



Who they are.



What they look like.



How they speak.



How they think.



What they believe.



What they fear.



What motivates them.



Who they care about.



Who they distrust.



What experiences shaped them.



Their current emotional and physical condition.



Relationships between characters should never simply exist as labels.



StoryOS should understand why two characters like one another, why they distrust one another, and how their shared history influences future interactions.



Characters should grow over time.



They should remember important experiences.



They should not suddenly change personality without explanation.



Growth should always have a reason.



\---



\# 5. Events



Events represent everything that happens within the universe.



Events are not chapters.



A chapter tells the reader a story.



An event records that something happened.



Examples include:



\* Wars.

\* Elections.

\* Discoveries.

\* Betrayals.

\* Conversations.

\* Marriages.

\* Deaths.

\* Political reforms.

\* Scientific breakthroughs.



Every event should record:



What happened.



Who participated.



Where it occurred.



When it occurred.



Why it happened.



What consequences followed.



This allows StoryOS to understand cause and effect rather than simply remembering isolated facts.



\---



\# 6. Chapters



Chapters represent the finished narrative produced by the Writer.



A chapter should contain:



Its number.



Its title.



The Director's objectives.



The completed narrative.



The important characters involved.



The important events introduced.



Any proposed Canon updates.



A chapter should never automatically become official history.



The chapter is the Writer's interpretation.



The Canon remains the official historical record.



Only approved information becomes permanent.



\---



\# 7. Dialogue Records



Dialogue is one of the most important parts of character identity.



StoryOS should preserve important conversations exactly as they were written.



Summaries are useful.



However, dialogue often captures personality better than any description.



For this reason, StoryOS should maintain dialogue records separately.



A dialogue record should contain:



Who spoke.



Who listened.



Where the conversation occurred.



When it occurred.



The exact conversation.



Its importance to the wider story.



These records allow future chapters to preserve a character's distinctive speaking style and recall significant promises, arguments or emotional moments.



This is intended to function like an intelligent version of the familiar "Ctrl + F" search, allowing both the user and the AI to locate previous conversations quickly.



\---



\# 8. Relationships



Relationships deserve their own structure.



A relationship is not simply:



"Friend."



Relationships change.



They possess history.



They contain trust, resentment, admiration, fear, loyalty and conflict.



StoryOS should understand both the public relationship and the private relationship between characters.



For example, two politicians may publicly appear friendly while secretly attempting to undermine one another.



The AI should understand both realities simultaneously.



\---



\# 9. Timeline



Every universe should possess a single official timeline.



The timeline records the chronological order of every important event.



This allows StoryOS to understand:



What happened first.



What happened afterwards.



How long has passed.



What events influenced later developments.



A timeline prevents contradictions and helps maintain long-term consistency across hundreds of chapters.



\---



\# 10. Canon Records



The Canon is the permanent memory of StoryOS.



Canon records represent information accepted as true.



The Canon should contain:



Historical events.



Approved character development.



Relationship changes.



Political developments.



World changes.



Important discoveries.



Permanent injuries.



Deaths.



New locations.



Any information not accepted into the Canon should remain separate until approved by the user.



This ensures that StoryOS always distinguishes between generated content and established history.



\---



\# 11. Search and Retrieval



StoryOS should treat searching as one of its core strengths.



Users should never need to manually browse hundreds of pages to find information.



Every important record should be searchable.



Searches should include:



Characters.



Countries.



Factions.



Events.



Dialogue.



Relationships.



Chapters.



Locations.



Keywords.



The purpose of searching is not simply convenience.



It is to allow both the user and the Writer to retrieve the exact information needed to maintain consistency throughout long-running stories.



\---



\# 12. Ownership of Information



Every piece of information should have one owner.



Ownership prevents duplication and contradiction.



For example:



The Timeline owns historical order.



The Character owns personality.



The Country owns political information.



The Faction owns organisational culture.



The Canon owns historical truth.



The Writer owns narrative prose.



The Director owns story direction.



No system should attempt to own information belonging to another.



This principle keeps StoryOS modular, organised and maintainable.



\---



\# 13. Long-Term Philosophy



StoryOS is designed around permanence.



The AI models used today will eventually change.



The software itself will continue to evolve.



However, the worlds created by users should remain valuable regardless of technological changes.



The purpose of this data model is therefore not simply to organise information.



Its purpose is to preserve fictional universes so that they remain understandable, searchable and expandable for many years.



Every story begins with imagination.



StoryOS exists to ensure that imagination is never lost.
